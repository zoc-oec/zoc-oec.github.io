Background & Rationale

High myopia (spherical equivalent ≤ −6.00 D) affects an estimated 170 million people worldwide and carries a substantially elevated risk of sight-threatening complications including myopic maculopathy, retinal detachment, glaucoma, and cataract — collectively the leading cause of irreversible vision loss in young adults in East Asia.

The ZHMC was established in partnership with the Brien Holden Vision Institute (BHVI) to generate the long-term natural history data needed to understand the onset and progression of myopic pathologies, identify predictors of visual impairment, and evaluate emerging myopia control interventions at scale.

The cohort recruits adults and children separately, enabling both cross-sectional characterization of disease spectrum and longitudinal tracking of axial elongation, macular structural changes, and visual functional decline over a decade.

Key Findings

📏

Axial Length Trajectories

The first 10-year axial elongation trajectory data in Chinese high myopes defined age-specific growth curves for children and adults, establishing the biometric basis for long-term intervention planning (JAMA Ophthalmol 2024).

💡

Red-Light Therapy Efficacy

ZHMC-embedded RCTs demonstrated that repeated low-level red-light (RLRL) therapy combined with orthokeratology substantially slows myopia progression and achieves axial shortening in some participants (Ophthalmology 2024; BJO 2026).

👁

Maculopathy Risk Prediction

Interpretable ML models trained on ZHMC data predict the 10-year risk of myopic maculopathy with high accuracy using baseline structural and biometric parameters, enabling early intervention in high-risk individuals.

📉

Visual Field Normative Data

ZHMC established myopia-specific normative databases for peripapillary RNFL thickness, resolving a critical clinical gap in detecting myopic glaucoma without misclassification due to axial-length-dependent artefacts (Ophthalmology 2023).
