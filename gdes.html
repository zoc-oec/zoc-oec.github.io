Background & Rationale

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) affects one in three adults with diabetes and is the leading cause of vision loss in working-age adults globally. Beyond the retina, diabetes causes widespread microvascular and metabolic damage. The eye provides a unique non-invasive window into systemic vascular health.

GDES was established to characterize the full spectrum of ocular phenotypes in type 2 diabetes — from early neurodegeneration and microvascular changes detectable by OCT and OCTA, to overt DR and diabetic macular edema — and to link these phenotypes with systemic biomarkers including metabolomics, proteomics, and genomic data.

This integrative approach enables discovery of novel biomarkers, mechanistic insights, and prediction models that extend beyond traditional DR grading to encompass systemic disease risk stratification.

Key Findings

🧬

Metabolomic Fingerprints

Plasma metabolomic profiles of retinal layer thicknesses predict type 2 diabetes risk and systemic disease mortality, extending the eye's role as a biomarker organ beyond ophthalmology (Nat Commun 2023, 2025).

🩸

Choriocapillaris as Biomarker

SS-OCTA-based choriocapillaris flow deficit predicts DR progression and DME development prospectively, providing a quantitative imaging biomarker for clinical risk stratification.

🫁

Eye–Kidney–Heart Axis

Optic nerve head OCTA parameters quantify renal dysfunction and predict renal function decline over 3 years, demonstrating the retinal vasculature as a window to systemic microvascular health.

📊

Proteome-DR Atlas

A proteome-wide atlas of DR mechanisms and risk (Nat Commun 2025) identifies novel druggable targets and substantially improves prediction of DR onset and progression beyond clinical risk factors.
