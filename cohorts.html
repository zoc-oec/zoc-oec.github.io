ZAP

Zhongshan Angle-Closure Prevention Trial

The Zhongshan Angle-Closure Prevention (ZAP) Trial is the world's largest and longest-running randomized controlled trial evaluating the efficacy of laser peripheral iridotomy (LPI) in preventing angle-closure glaucoma among individuals with primary angle-closure suspect (PACS) â a condition affecting an estimated 500 million people globally. Initiated at Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center in 2008, ZAP has now accumulated 18 continuous years of prospective follow-up, making it the definitive longitudinal dataset on angle-closure disease natural history and prevention.

The landmark Lancet 2019 publication (He M et al.) established that LPI significantly reduces the risk of angle-closure progression, fundamentally reshaping international clinical guidelines. Subsequent 14-year extended follow-up analyses (Ophthalmology 2023; The Innovation Medicine 2023) confirmed the durability of the LPI effect and characterized the biometric risk factors â including anterior chamber depth, iris thickness, and lens vault â that predict progression. The 2024 and 2026 JAMA Ophthalmology papers further used AS-OCTâderived trabecular-iris contact to quantify structural closure mechanisms over time.

Scientific Distinction
ZAP holds the longest continuous follow-up of any angle-closure prevention cohort in the world. Its unique within-person design â one treated eye, one untreated eye â eliminates confounding from inter-individual variation, providing unparalleled causal inference power.

The ZAP biobank contains repeated gonioscopy, AS-OCT biometric series, intraocular pressure trajectories, visual field data, and BCVA records across 18 annual follow-up cycles. These data have underpinned predictive algorithms for glaucoma conversion adopted by the World Glaucoma Association. Ongoing analyses are characterizing the 18-year cumulative incidence of angle-closure and primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG) â data with direct policy implications for population screening programmes across Asia.

GDES

Guangzhou Diabetic Eye Study

The Guangzhou Diabetic Eye Study (GDES) is a large-scale prospective longitudinal cohort of adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus, established at Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center between 2017 and 2019. With over 3,000 participants aged 35–85 years and now entering its 7th year of continuous follow-up, GDES is one of the most deeply phenotyped diabetic eye disease cohorts in the world, integrating multimodal retinal imaging with a comprehensive multi-omics biobank.

What sets GDES apart is its unprecedented depth of molecular profiling. Plasma samples were analysed using Olink proximity extension assay to quantify 2,923 plasma proteins, making GDES one of the largest proteomics-linked ophthalmic cohorts globally. Alongside proteomics, the cohort has generated metabolomics profiles, genome-wide genotyping, and longitudinal OCT/SS-OCTA measurements at multiple timepoints, enabling integrative multi-omics analyses linking retinal phenotypes to systemic disease mechanisms.

Multi-omics Powerhouse
GDES links 2,923-protein plasma proteomics (Olink) + metabolomics + genome-wide genotyping to multimodal retinal imaging (OCT, SS-OCTA, fundus photos) â enabling discovery of systemic biomarkers of diabetic complications at a scale unmatched in ophthalmology.

GDES has produced landmark publications in Nature Communications (2023, 2025) and PLOS Medicine (2026) demonstrating that retinal biomarkers derived from AI-metabolomics profiling predict mortality and cardiometabolic risk beyond conventional clinical factors. The 2025 proteomics atlas (Nat Commun) identified 71 plasma proteins associated with diabetic retinal neurodegeneration across inflammatory and extracellular-matrix pathways. Longitudinal OCTA analyses have defined the earliest structural signs of diabetic retinopathy and linked foveal avascular zone changes to renal and cardiovascular outcomes.
