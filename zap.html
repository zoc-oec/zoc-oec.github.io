Background & Rationale

Primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG) is the leading cause of irreversible blindness in Asia, affecting an estimated 23 million people worldwide. Identification of individuals at risk — those with primary angle-closure suspect (PACS) anatomy — offers a window for preventive intervention before glaucoma develops.

Laser peripheral iridotomy (LPI) creates a small opening in the iris to relieve pupil block and widen the drainage angle. While widely used, the efficacy of LPI in truly preventing glaucoma in PACS was uncertain before ZAP.

ZAP was designed as a paired-eye randomized trial in which one eye per participant receives LPI and the fellow eye serves as control, providing a rigorous within-person comparison that controls for systemic and genetic confounders.

Study Design

Eligibility
Adults 50–70 years; bilateral PACS by gonioscopy; no prior LPI or angle surgery
Randomization
One eye randomized to LPI; fellow eye observation (within-person control)
Primary Endpoint
Progression to primary angle closure (PAC) or primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG)
Site
Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center, Guangzhou, China · Initiated 2008

Key Findings

📉

LPI Reduces Progression

LPI significantly reduced 6-year progression from PACS to PAC or PACG compared to observation. The landmark Lancet 2019 paper established LPI's preventive role in high-risk Asian eyes.

🔬

Biometric Predictors Identified

14-year follow-up revealed that ocular biometrics (anterior chamber depth, lens vault, iris volume dynamics) predict which PACS eyes progress despite LPI, enabling targeted risk stratification.

⏱

Durable Protection

Protection from LPI was maintained through 14 years of follow-up (Ophthalmology 2023), the longest RCT follow-up data for angle closure prevention in the literature.

🤖

AI Risk Prediction

Machine learning models trained on ZAP biometric data predict angle closure progression with high accuracy, enabling individualized treatment planning beyond traditional grading schemes.
